INSIGHTS — LILA BLACK Data Analysis

All stats come from 797 matches over 5 days (Feb 10–14), across three maps.

---

Insight 1: AmbroseValley is basically a kill funnel

What stood out:
AmbroseValley shows heavy kill clustering in just a few central zones, while player movement is spread everywhere. GrandRift, on the other hand, has similar movement but almost no fights.

Key takeaway:
Players aren’t more aggressive on one map vs another — they’re just being forced into fights by the map layout.

Why it matters:
If one map becomes “the fighting map,” the others start feeling like empty space. That hurts variety and long-term engagement.

What to do:
- Break up chokepoints in AmbroseValley
- Add incentives (loot/objectives) in GrandRift to force encounters

---

Insight 2: A lot of matches end with no combat

What stood out:
Roughly 1 in 3 matches on Lockdown and GrandRift end without a single kill for the player.

What’s happening:
Players are able to move around safely without ever running into conflict — especially by sticking to outer areas.

Why it matters:
A match with no tension isn’t really a game loop — it’s just movement. If this happens too often, players lose interest.

What to do:
- Reduce “safe pathing” around the edges
- Push players inward earlier (zone pressure, objectives, etc.)
- Add early-game hotspots to force interaction

---

Insight 3: Loot placement is quietly controlling combat

What stood out:
AmbroseValley has way more loot — but more importantly, loot and kills happen in the same places.

What that means:
Players run into each other because they’re chasing the same resources.

Why it matters:
Loot isn’t just economy — it’s a tool for shaping where fights happen.

What to do:
- Increase loot density in key Lockdown areas to drive encounters
- Identify high-overlap zones (loot + kills) and refine those first
- Study GrandRift — it has fewer resources but still produces fights

---

Core idea

Where players go = where loot is  
Where players meet = where fights happen
